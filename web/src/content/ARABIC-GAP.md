# Arabic gap list — what `Approved Arabic Content.v1` does not cover

The client's spreadsheet translates body copy. It does **not** translate the strings
that live in its own Section Title column: the section eyebrows, the item titles and
the quality-plan stage names. Those are among the most visible words on the page, so
they are listed here rather than quietly left behind.

Every string below **keeps the in-house Arabic it already had** — reviewed, and no worse
than before. Nothing here was invented this round. Paste the English into a v2 of the
spreadsheet to have the copywriter cover them.

## Two questions for the client, not gaps

**1. Client and partner names.** The sheet's Arabic cell for the logo wall is the Latin
names, unchanged. The site currently Arabises them in the logos' `alt` text
(`مجموعة بن لادن السعودية`, `هيلتون`, `نسما وشركاه`) — text that exists only for a screen
reader. Applying the sheet literally would put Latin brand names back into the Arabic
accessibility tree, so it was **not** applied. Confirm which the client wants.

**2. `landing.about.heading` ends in two full stops** — `التصنيع ليس سوى البداية..` —
where the only other pause in the deck uses three (`الغرفة... منظومة`). Shipped exactly as
approved rather than silently corrected. Almost certainly a typo worth fixing in v2.

## Out of scope, deliberately

Alt text and ARIA labels (49 strings), logo file slugs (24), and the site chrome, contact
form, error states and SEO metadata (60). None is marketing copy, and the sheet never
claimed them.
